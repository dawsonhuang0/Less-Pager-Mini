Two different stress tests. They fail in different ways, and the
first is the one that kills a backtracking engine.

    tests/redos-long.txt    10k / 50k / 200k / 1M characters ON ONE LINE
    tests/redos-many.txt    2,000,000 short lines (82 MB)

The two data files are NOT kept in the tree - they are 40 MB of the
letter a. Rebuild them when you need them:

    python3 - <<'EOF'
    with open('tests/redos-long.txt','w') as f:
        f.write('=== one long line per section; length is what kills a backtracker ===\n')
        for n in (10000, 50000, 200000, 1000000):
            f.write('len%d %s\n' % (n, 'a' * n))
    with open('tests/redos-many.txt','w') as f:
        for i in range(2000000):
            f.write('line%d %s\n' % (i, 'a' * 80))
    EOF

Backtracking blows up with the LENGTH of a single line, not with how
many lines there are. A million short lines is a million cheap calls -
linear, just slow. One million-character line is where an exponential
engine never returns. 4000 characters was not enough to trouble
anything, which is why these go to 1M.

    node dist/cli.js -X tests/redos-long.txt
    node dist/cli.js -X --use-js-regexp tests/redos-long.txt
    ./less/less -X tests/redos-long.txt

ON THE LONG LINES — type each with / and RETURN:

  (a+)+b            classic nested quantifier
  (a*)*b            empty-loop variant
  (a|a)*b           alternation of the same thing
  (a|aa)+b          overlapping alternatives
  (x+x+)+y          the textbook one
  ([a-z]+)+b        class instead of a literal
  (.*a){20}b        wildcard blowup
  a{100}{100}       nested counted repetition

  a+b               control: should be instant everywhere
  len1000000        control: a plain literal on the same line

ON THE MANY LINES — throughput, and whether you can get out:

  zzzznotfound      scans all 2M lines and finds nothing
  line 1999999      the match is on the very last line
  aaaa              matches every line
  (a+)+b            2M cheap calls; slow, but should not hang

WHAT TO WATCH

  1. Does it come back at all, and how fast.
  2. Does ^C abort a slow search. og checks ABORT_SIGS between LINES
     (search.c), so it can stop between lines but never inside one
     regexec call - on redos-long.txt a single line may be
     uninterruptible even in og.
  3. Does the pager still work afterwards, or is the screen wedged.
  4. If og hangs too, that is worth knowing: it means the limit is
     the C library's, not ours.
